\section{The centered difference}
Let $u$ have six continuous derivatives near $x$ and let $h>0$.
We define the centered second difference by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stencil}
 D_h u(x)=\frac{u(x+h)-2u(x)+u(x-h)}{h^2}.
\end{equation}
The symmetric numerator cancels its odd powers of $h$.
The Taylor expansion therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
 D_h u(x)=u''(x)+\frac{h}{12}u^{(4)}(x)+O(h^4).
\end{equation}
The leading error is second order in $h$.

\section{A polynomial check}
For $u(x)=x^4$ at $x=1$ with $h=1/2$, direct substitution into
\eqref{eq:stencil} gives $D_hu(1)=25/2$.
The exact second derivative is $u''(1)=12$.
The difference between these values is the leading error term.
